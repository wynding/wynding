## What M4 is

M4 is **depth** (roadmap; PRD 0001's activation row): **tower leveling**, so a tower can be made
stronger where it stands, and **creep leveling** as the per-wave creep HP curve, now tuned
against it. It lands on M3's finished campaign: the same Crossing board, the same 30 waves, the
same nine towers and eight creeps. It is a public milestone build — the **`p1-m4` checkpoint**,
not a release — under the full accessibility gate (ADR 0003) and externalized strings (ADR
0004), like every milestone.

**Why it matters now.** M3's builds end the run holding about a third of their income. The
strongest reference build, the serpentine, spends 799 of its 1,286 and wins holding 487: its bank
climbs from 26 at wave 23 to 487 at wave 30, because once the board is full there is nothing left
to buy. The other two winners finish holding 537 and 354, and the block-3 losers die holding
283–412. Upgrades spend that bank, so they move every outcome M3 pinned, and they move it mostly
in block 3, where the bank is. M3 expected this: "tuned" meant structurally sound, with M4's
leveling reshaping the curve (m3.md).

**M3's rulings carry into M4 unchanged** (Rob, 2026-11-26): 30 waves in three blocks of ten, 20
lives, the Crossing board, tower costs at the first pass × 1.2 with a starting bounty of 96,
juggling allowed and never required, and "tuned" meaning structurally sound rather than final.
M5 still re-tunes the economy and adds Easy and Hard.

The grill settled six things the roadmap and PRD left open:

- **Three levels on one straight track, for the seven attacking towers.** Level 1 is today's
  tower, and two upgrades take it to level 3. A level raises only how strong the tower is, never
  what it is. The beacon and the mine stay single-level.
- **An upgrade costs the tower's base cost, then twice it,** and it is instant: the tower fires at
  its new level from its next shot.
- **Creep leveling stays the creep HP axis.** Speed, armor and bounty stay part of each creep's
  identity. What changes is that creep HP keeps rising past the board-full wave instead of
  flattening, so upgrading is required to win block 3.
- **Targeting stays sticky "first",** deferred and measured. The checker gains a boss-screening
  probe, and selectable targeting ships only if the probe shows screening that M3's content fixes
  can't cure.
- **"Upgrade value" is retired as a score input.** The score formula is unchanged.
- **Anti-air stays parked.** `antiair`'s own ladder is the expected relief, and the checker
  re-reports anti-air spend with upgrades in play.

Terminology is the glossary's ([CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md)); this milestone's grill added
**Level** there: a tower's position on its upgrade track, a word that belongs to towers alone and
never names a board, a wave, a difficulty tier or a star grade.

## Concrete spec

### Tower leveling

**The shape** (Rob, 2026-11-26):

- **Three levels on one linear track.** Level 1 is the tower as built. Each upgrade raises it one
  level, to at most level 3. There is no branch and no choice of path.
- **A level raises only the tower's headline magnitude:** its damage, its slow strength, its
  poison or its stun chance (the ladder table below). It never changes the tower's effect kinds or
  their order, its target domain, cadence, range, shot travel, blast radius or any duration. So a
  tower's role, its Panel role line and the colour of its head never change with its level.
- **The seven attacking cadenced towers level:** `basic`, `slow`, `splash`, `venom`, `stun`,
  `antiair` and `frost-splash`. The **`beacon`** and the **`mine`** stay single-level, and their
  Panel keeps today's inert "Max level" control and its screen-reader text.
- **Instant.** An accepted upgrade has no timer and costs no shot. The tower fires at its new
  level from its next shot, and its target lock and cooldown are untouched.

Why this shape:

- **Three levels** give 14 upgrade steps to tune and present, read as "Level 2 of 3" on a phone's
  Panel, and leave a sink the late bank can't fill (see Board-full and the upgrade sink).
- **Magnitudes only.** A tower's role is presentation over its effect primitives (glossary), so a
  level that added an effect kind would change the role mid-life. A level that added air would
  make flyers a question of which upgrade you bought rather than what you built.
- **Fixed cadence and travel keep the sim's per-tower bounds.** Shot travel shorter than cadence
  keeps at most one impact in flight per tower; the DoT duration-to-cadence ratio of at most 8
  sizes the DoT record rail; and fires per tick set `step()`'s cost under ADR 0005. A level that
  changes magnitudes only leaves all three untouched.
- **The beacon and the mine.** The beacon's aura multiplies its neighbours' damage at fire time, so
  it already strengthens their upgrades. A mine is consumed when it fires, so upgrading one would
  be the same decision as buying another.
- **Instant, because build-while-paused already lets an upgrade land on any sim moment.** A
  real-time timer would mean nothing, and a sim-time one would be a cost rather than a skill and
  would need a new "upgrading, can't fire" cue. Wynding's real-time skill is re-mazing, and the
  base experience never requires time pressure (PRD 0001 §9).

A slow stays above the ruleset's ¼ speed floor at every level. A stun-chance ladder keeps ADR
0010's frozen draw sequence: the roll draws once per stun attempt on a creep that isn't immune,
whatever the chance, so raising the chance changes no draw count or order.

**The cost curve** (Rob, 2026-11-26). Each upgrade costs the tower's base cost, then twice it, for
cumulative costs of 1×, 2× and 4× base (`basic` 6 / 12 / 24). Damage grows about 1.75×, then 3×.

For the five towers whose level grows damage (`basic`, `antiair`, `splash`, `frost-splash` and
`venom`'s poison):

- **Against an unarmored creep** an upgrade buys a little less damage per bounty than a new tower
  (about 0.85×, then 0.75×).
- **Against armor it buys more,** because armor is a flat per-hit reduction: 1.4–2.75× per bounty
  at levels 2–3. `basic` against the `boss` goes from 2 damage a hit to 9 and then 22, which is 11×
  the damage for 4× the bounty. A level-3 `antiair` kills an `armored-flyer` at 270% in 5 hits
  instead of 27.
- **An upgrade always buys more damage per cell** than a new tower.

A `slow` or `stun` upgrade buys control, not damage: a stronger slow or a likelier stun.

So a player fills the board first, upgrades where the maze folds past a tower, and upgrades early
only to answer an armored wave. That keeps M3's tuning of blocks 1–2 and its board-full wave near
23, and it puts the new decisions where the bank is.

**First-pass ladders** — the tuning surface. Cadence, range, travel, blast radius and every
duration are unchanged at every level.

### Format 4: a tower's `levels`

- **A tower may carry an optional `levels` list:** its upgrades, in order, one or two entries
  (never an empty list). Each entry restates the
  step's `cost` and the tower's full `effects` list at that level. A tower without `levels` is
  single-level.
- **A level differs from level 1 in magnitudes only.** Its effects have the same kinds, in the same
  order and with the same forms, as level 1's. Only these may differ: direct and blast `damage`, a
  slow's `mulFp`, a DoT's `damagePerTick` and a stun's `chanceNum`. Every other effect key — blast
  radius, every duration, a DoT's own cadence — equals level 1's.
- **The attack is the tower's own.** Domain, range, cadence and travel sit on the tower, not on its
  levels, so a level that changes them can't be written at all rather than merely isn't (the
  precedent of a capability profile kept narrower than the schema).
- **Every level passes the capability checks a tower does,** and every step cost is a positive
  integer.
- **M4's capability profile admits `levels` only on a cadenced attacking tower, and at most two
  entries** (three levels). A support or burst tower with `levels` is rejected at compile time.
- **`formatVersion` 3 → 4.** The validator rejects unknown keys, so a format bump is the honest
  signal (the M3 precedent).

### How the sim holds a level

**A tower's level is read from its spend.** A tower's `spend` is already its cumulative
investment, stored so that refunds stay forward-compatible when upgrades land (`tower.ts`), and
the sell path already refunds ⌊¾ × spend⌋. Today a tower row is valid only if its spend exactly
equals its catalog entry's cost. M4 widens that to "equals one of the tower's cumulative level
costs", and the tower's level is the one whose cumulative cost it equals. Step costs are positive,
so the reading is unique.

The tower row gains **no column**, so a log with no upgrade command reproduces `simVersion` 18 tick
for tick, world-hash included. Combat, the fire-time snapshot and the aura read the level's
effects; nothing else about a tower changes.

### The upgrade command

`upgradeTower { tower }` names a tower by entity id, as `sellTower` does, and raises it one level.
It is **accepted** iff the tower is valid, it has a next level, and bounty covers the step (the
same affordability rule as a placement). Otherwise it is a **deterministic no-op**. A `tower` that
isn't a safe integer makes the replay invalid (ADR 0006: malformed → invalid, illegal → no-op).

- **Order:** commands apply in input order on their tick. An upgrade then a sell on the same tick
  refunds ¾ of the upgraded spend.
- **Timing:** legal whenever placement is, including before Start and while paused.
- **One level per command.** Each command's price is its step, the validator's check stays
  trivial, and two presses in the Panel buy two levels.
- **What an upgrade never touches:** an impact already in flight keeps the damage and effects it
  left with (PRD 0001 §4). A DoT the same tower re-applies adopts its new magnitude on its next hit
  (PRD 0001 §4), and the beacon's aura multiplies the level's damage at fire time.

### Selling, refunds and juggling

**The rule is unchanged:** a sell refunds ⌊¾ × cumulative spend⌋, base plus upgrades (PRD 0001 §3).
There is still no move action. Selling and rebuilding starts the new tower at level 1, and
re-upgrading pays every step again, so moving an upgraded tower costs a quarter of its value: a
level-3 `basic` (24) refunds 18. That makes the PRD's picture concrete: the player mazes with
cheap towers and parks upgraded firepower in stable spots.

**Juggling is unchanged.** It uses level-1 walls, so a `basic` flip still nets 2 bounty. It stays
allowed and never required: every winner wins without a sell while a creep is alive, and the
juggling reference build is re-reported with upgrades.

### Creep leveling: the creep HP curve, un-flattened

**Creep leveling is the per-wave creep HP axis M3 introduced** (Rob, 2026-11-26): one whole-number
`hpPercent` per wave, applied at spawn as `max(1, ⌊catalog HP × hpPercent / 100⌋)`, authored data
that never reacts to the player's maze. No other axis levels:

- **Speed stays per kind.** A speed ramp would divide the value of the maze every wave, and the
  maze is the player's main skill.
- **Armor stays per kind.** Armor is flat per hit, so growing it zeroes the small hits first: +3
  armor zeroes a level-1 `antiair` against `armored-flyer`, and `antiair` is the only tower that
  meaningfully damages that creep. Armor already gives upgrades their job, because the arc's
  armored kinds are where a level pays more than a new tower.
- **Bounty stays per kind.** Whether it grows is M5's economy.

**The curve no longer flattens.** M3 flattened it at the board-full wave "because nothing in M3
makes a tower stronger once there is no room left" (m3.md). Tower leveling is that thing, so the
flattening rule retires and waves 24–30 become the main tuning surface. Blocks 1–2 keep M3's
`hpPercent` unless the checker's pressure profile flattens. Block 3, first pass:

| Wave | Role (m3.md)                      | M3 `hpPercent` | M4 first pass |
| ---- | --------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| 21   | relief                            | 195            | 195           |
| 22   | density, both routes              | 215            | 215           |
| 23   | speed, both routes                | 235            | 235           |
| 24   | armor, both routes                | 270            | 270           |
| 25   | combined                          | 270            | 290           |
| 26   | air — both lanes at once          | 270            | 310           |
| 27   | combined                          | 270            | 330           |
| 28   | combined                          | 270            | 350           |
| 29   | wall — armored flyers, both lanes | 270            | 370           |
| 30   | boss finale                       | 270            | 390           |

Over waves 24–30 this raises the creep HP a run must kill from 13,472 to 17,320 (+29%). The rise
is uniform and moderate because upgrades buy below par against the unarmored waves (25 and 27) and
well above it against the armored, air and boss waves (24, 26 and 28–30); +20 a wave sits between
the two. The wave preview is unchanged: its creep HP clause already states each wave's
`hpPercent` ("+290% hit points" at wave 30).

**The finale hardens** (Rob, 2026-11-26): upgrading is required to win block 3. That keeps the
first-attempt target M3 set, in which a player who knows the genre but not Wynding usually loses
the first run somewhere in waves 21–30.

### Targeting

**Unchanged in M4: sticky "first"** (PRD 0001 §4, measured per destination as in m3.md).
Selectable targeting priority is deferred, and the question that would justify it, escorts
screening a boss, is settled by measurement (Rob, 2026-11-26):

1. **Story 1 adds a boss-screening probe** to section B: per boss, the ticks it spent in range of a
   tower that could hit it while every such tower held another target.
2. **If screening shows up** — more than a token share of a boss's in-range time, or Rob sees it in
   his runs — **M3's named content fixes come first:** a longer escort offset, or a slower escort
   (m3.md's flags).
3. **Only if those fail does a per-tower choice ship,** in its smallest form: two settings, "first"
   and "strongest" (the most current hit points; ties go to "first", then the lower id), both
   sticky. It would be a `setTargeting { tower, priority }` command counted in the tower-command
   budget, one tower column appended last (which moves every hash), and a two-state Panel control
   with a rebindable key and full and glance strings. Decided before story 2 lands, it rides story
   2's `simVersion` bump; decided later, it takes its own.
4. **The probe runs twice:** in story 1 on the M3 fixture, an early reading, and again at the
   balance pass on M4's final arc, since upgrades and block 3's higher creep hit points change how
   bosses and escorts meet the towers. The content fixes in step 2 change only `rulesetHash`, and
   the balance pass makes them if the second reading calls for them. If only the second reading
   shows screening and those fixes don't cure it, the balance pass reports it to Rob at close-out
   as a follow-up: a per-tower choice is not built inside M4 after story 2.

**Why defer.** PRD 0001 makes selectable priority optional for M4. Rob's M3 runs raised no
screening, and nothing has measured it. A targeting setting would cost a tower column (losing the
no-new-column parity above), a control in a Compact Panel that is already full, and a new degree
of freedom in every reference build.

### Anti-air

**Parked** (Rob, 2026-11-26). M3 recorded that its winners put 15–30% of their spend into
flyer-only towers (up to 44% counting every tower that hits air), as evidence for the tower
catalog. Only `slow` (both domains) and `antiair` (air only) hit flyers. M4 expects `antiair`'s
own ladder to relieve that: maxing all 16 of the serpentine's `antiair` costs 432 of its 487
surplus, and each one then kills an armored flyer in 5 hits instead of 27. The both-domain `basic`
stays in the backlog where Rob put it (m2.md), and the checker reports the anti-air shares with
upgrades in play beside M3's.

### Board-full and the upgrade sink

- **Board-full keeps its definition and target:** the wave where the strongest reference build
  places its last tower, about wave 24 ± 2 (measured at 23 in M3). It no longer sets where the
  creep HP curve flattens.
- **After board-full, the bounty decision is what to upgrade.** The last waves are about holding
  what you built, and investing in it. Waves 24–30 pay 461–500 bounty on their own, which is the
  bounty upgrades exist to absorb.
- **The sink never saturates.** Taking every tower of the strongest build that can level to level
  3 costs about 2,070 bounty on the first-pass ladders. The surplus, 487 with no early calls and 644
  with every one, covers 23–31% of it, so "which few towers do I max" stays a real choice.
- **Upgrade headroom is reported:** the strongest build's bank at its win, and how many of its
  towers reach level 3. The intended shape is a bank no larger than about one late wave's income
  (≈ 50–120), with not every tower maxed. The sink should absorb the surplus but never fill.

### Economy

**No existing economy number moves.** Starting bounty 96, the tower costs (×1.2), the ¾ refund, the
clear bonuses, the early-call divisor and the kill bounties all stay as M3 tuned them; the
ladders' step costs are the only new numbers. M5 re-tunes the economy against the final content,
so holding it fixed now leaves M4 two moving surfaces: the ladders and the curve. **If the checker
can't hold board-full near 24 with upgrades in, the step costs move first, never the base costs.**

### Lives, scoring & star grade

**Unchanged:** 20 lives; win: `Σ kill bounties + Σ early-call credits + max(0, lives) ×
survivalMul` (25); loss: 0; star cutoffs `[1, 12, 18]` lives left. The spread of 3★ and ≤ 2★
winners is kept by the curve, not by re-cutting stars.

**"Upgrade value" is retired as a separate score input** (Rob, 2026-11-26; PRD 0001 §7 as
amended):

- **Upgrades already earn score through what they produce.** A creep pays the same bounty however
  it dies, and winners kill nearly every creep, so kill bounty is nearly constant among them and
  the score separates them by lives (25 each) and early calls. Both are outcomes that good upgrading buys.
- **Any measure of upgrade spend rewards the input, not the outcome.** It would make "spend the
  bank on upgrades before the last creep dies" a rote end-of-run chore worth about 240–480 score
  at the cleanest measures, the size of the 500 survival ceiling.
- **It would penalise mines** (consumed) and juggling's refunds, and it would pull against the
  efficiency input M5 adds. Whether spending should score against banking is revisited with that
  efficiency input.

No grading change, so no `simVersion` cost.

### Command bounds

PRD 0001's command-bounds clause requires the per-tick cap to admit a full legitimate paused
re-maze. **At 64 it doesn't:** pause runs no ticks, so a whole pause lands on
one tick, and re-mazing the serpentine's 83 towers is 166 commands (each sold, each placed again).
Upgrades make large paused bursts routine: one level on every tower of that build is 83 commands.
**512** admits a re-maze of the board's whole 143-tower capacity (286 commands) plus a level on
every rebuilt tower (429). A rebuild of the whole capacity to level 3 would be 572 commands, but no
run can pay for it: 143 level-3 `basic`s cost 3,432 against a whole-run income of at most 1,443.

| Bound                      | M4                                 | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `MAX_TOTAL_TOWER_COMMANDS` | 2,000, now counting `upgradeTower` | each upgrade finds its tower through the full validity walk, and a rejected one is a free no-op the economy can't rate-limit — the reasoning that put `sellTower` in it. Every committed command still destroys at least 1 bounty, so commands ≤ I = 1,443 (below) |
| `MAX_TOWER_PLACEMENTS`     | 1,000, unchanged                   | an upgrade mints no tower id                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `MAX_DOT_RECORDS`          | 9,000, unchanged                   | DoT sources are placements, and DoT durations and cadences are fixed per level                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `MAX_IN_FLIGHT_IMPACTS`    | unchanged                          | cadence and travel are fixed per level                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `MAX_INPUTS_PER_TICK`      | 64 → 512                           | a full paused re-maze, as above; the client's cap is the same constant and follows                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `MAX_MATCH_TICKS`          | 72,000, unchanged                  | the compile check reads levels and still fits (below)                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `AOE_SCAN_CEILING`         | unchanged                          | spawns are unchanged                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

- **The per-tick cap rides the bump.** It is a validator bound, and by the `AOE_SCAN_CEILING`
  precedent (`packages/sim/src/ruleset.ts`) changing which logs validate is a behaviour change, so
  it moves with story 2's `simVersion` bump.
- **Every committed command still destroys bounty.** The worst churn under the first-pass ladders,
  `basic` place → upgrade → sell, nets 3 bounty over 3 commands, exactly 1 a command, so a run's
  commands can't exceed its income I = 1,443 (96 starting + 1,088 kills + 102 clear bonuses + 157
  early calls) < 2,000. Story 2 rewrites the budget derivation in `ruleset-shared.ts`, which still
  quotes M3's first-pass I = 1,423 and a 5-bounty `basic`, and extends `tower-budgets.test.ts` with
  "every place → upgrade(s) → sell cycle nets at least 1 bounty a command".
- **The run-length compile check reads every level.** It assumes every slowable creep walks under the
  catalog's strongest slow, so it must take the strongest slow at any level, not level 1's. With
  the first-pass level-3 `slow` (×0.30), the `boss`'s slowed walk grows from 27,030 ticks to
  48,653, and the check reads 17,790 + 48,653 = 66,443 against the 72,000 cap. The ¼ speed floor
  already holds the boss at that speed, so no slow ladder can push this arc past the cap.

### Perf

**No new perf budget work.** ADR 0005's stress scene stays unchanged. The campaign perf scene is
re-recorded with M4's strongest build at the balance pass, **recorded, not gated** (the real-device
pass, #141, keeps the final word).

- **Per-tick sim work grows only by the level read.** With cadence, range and travel fixed per
  level, upgrades change no fires, scans or impacts per tick, and targeting stays one pass of
  towers × live creeps. Reading a tower's level is at most three comparisons.
- **How many creeps are alive can move either way.** Damage upgrades kill sooner; slow and stun
  upgrades hold creeps in the maze longer. The re-recorded campaign scene measures it. M3's
  serpentine peaked at 26 alive, against ADR 0005's 300-creep, 150-tower stress scene.
- **The stress scene keeps level-1 towers.** Upgraded damage would thin its 300 live creeps and
  weaken the ceiling it exists to hold. It still gates the level code: every tower, level 1
  included, reads its effects from a per-level table built once when the ruleset compiles, so a
  level-1 shot and a level-3 shot take the same path and differ only in the numbers they read.
- **The risk is render, not sim.** A level mark on every tower must add no draw cost, so the cue is
  baked into the tower art rather than drawn as extra marks each time the board renders.

If range or cadence ever becomes a level stat, re-measure: fires per tick and blast scans change.

### Presentation, accessibility & i18n

The Panel already has the slot: every selected tower's Panel carries Sell beside an inert,
focusable, `aria-disabled` "Max level" control. M4 makes it live rather than inventing an
affordance.

- **The Panel names the level:** "Level 2 of 3" in the full text, with a short glance form.
- **The Upgrade control goes live:** "Upgrade" with its step cost, the bounty gem beside the text
  and never inside the string (the Panel's own rule for every cost).
  - Unaffordable, it stays focusable and `aria-disabled`, with its reason ("needs 12").
  - At level 3 it reads "Max level", as today. The beacon and the mine keep today's control and
    screen-reader text.
- **Next-level preview.** Each stat row the next level changes shows the change in all three Panel
  forms: the full sentences (the accessible text), the glance, and the label-value pairs. For
  example: "Damage: 10, 17 at level 2".
- **Sell rises with the level.** Its live refund already patches in place.
- **A paused upgrade shows at once** in the Panel, the bounty chip and the board, as a pending build
  or sell does.
- **Announcements:** the live region announces each upgrade and each rejection.
- **On-board cue:** a tower's level shows as a shape — one mark per level gained, static, at least
  3:1 contrast, never colour alone (ADR 0003 §2) — baked into the tower art.
- **Input:**
  - **Keyboard:** a rebindable `upgrade` action on a free key. The settings list picks it up from

  - **Touch:** the control is at least 44 × 44 CSS px (ADR 0003), and there is no press-and-hold to

  already height-capped and scrolls, and its heading, Sell, Max level and Close already need 220 px

- **Every new string externalized** per ADR 0004, each variant its own literal key so `i18n-check`

## What tuned means (the acceptance bar)

M4's arc is **tuned** when everything in A holds in CI, B is reported, and Rob signs off on C. Every
threshold below is first pass, and the checker's to move.

**A. Gated (deterministic, against the shipped bundle):**

1. **At least three reference winning builds,** structurally distinct by M2's rule, with spend by
   tower id counted cumulatively (base plus upgrades) so the rule still reads where the bounty
   went. At least one **merges both routes into one maze** and one **defends them separately**; at
   least one wins **3★** and at least one wins **≤ 2★**; at least one puts **at least 40% of its
   spend into upgrades** and one **at most 15%**. Every winner upgrades, and **all of them win
   without a sell while any creep is alive.**
2. **M3's five pinned losers, re-pinned at their windows,** each located by creep id: _undefended_
   by wave 3; _west only_ within two waves of the north route opening; _no anti-air_ at an air wave
   by the end of block 2; _damage only_ and the _competent corridor_ to a block-3 creep. **Every
   loser except _undefended_ dies holding at most about 10% of all the bounty it received** (its
   bank at the loss against its total income). Measured as the bank, selling and rebuying can't
   inflate it. Under M4 a loser that dies on a bank has declined to play. The early
   losers already clear it (west only and no anti-air die holding 0 and 1), so the floor bites the
   block-3 losers, which die holding 309 and 412 at M3. M3's other plausibility floors still apply.
3. **Upgrades are required for block 3:** M3's three winners as drawn, never upgrading, each
   lose on M4's arc, to a creep of waves 21–30.
4. **A dominant-strategy guard:** a build of only `basic` and `antiair`, taken to level 3, must not
   win 3★.
5. **Budgets:** the bundle compiles under the 72,000-tick cap; total spawns ≤ 2,000; peak creeps
   alive ≤ 200 in every reference build; every build's tower commands, upgrades included, inside
   2,000, and its placements inside 1,000.
6. **The M4 determinism golden:** the best upgrading reference build's `finalHash` and
   `traceDigest`, with an engagement probe (an upgrade accepted, a boss damaged, wave 30 reached,
   terminal = win), joined to the determinism-version guard's watch set.

**B. Reported, owner-reviewed, not gated:** M3's lines — the per-wave pressure profile for every
reference build, the board-full wave, the juggling build beside the honest winners, the
centre-only anti-air build, and the campaign perf scene on both pinned device profiles — plus:

- **upgrade spend** per build, and the **bank at the win**;
- **upgrade headroom:** the strongest build's bank at its win and its count of level-3 towers;
- **the boss-screening line** (see Targeting);
- **the anti-air shares** — flyer-only, and every tower that hits air — beside M3's 18 / 30 / 15%.

**C. The owner gate.** Rob plays the shipped arc to a result at least three times — at least one
win and one loss — and signs off. This is the standing balance gate (working agreement). Three
questions for his runs:

1. Did you find and use upgrades without being told?
2. Did waves 24–30 feel like holding and investing, rather than waiting?
3. Did you ever feel forced to sell an upgraded tower to re-maze?

**The target a first attempt should feel is M3's, unchanged:** a player who knows the genre but not
Wynding usually loses the first run somewhere in waves 21–30; a competent second or third attempt

winners are this target in checkable form.

**The UAT ledger.** A tracking issue logs every autonomous call M4 makes — step numbers, strings,

**Outside players** (Rob, 2026-11-26). If it's easy for Rob, two or three players who know the

## Determinism & data

- **Layering unchanged:** render and input read sim state and never mutate it; gameplay stays a
  pure function of `(seed, ruleset, boardId, tickInputs)` under the replay's stamped `simVersion`.
- **`formatVersion` 3 → 4** at story 2, for a tower's `levels`.
- **`simVersion` 18 → 19** at story 2, the one bump M4 plans. It carries the widened validity rule; level-resolved
  stats in combat, the aura and the snapshot; the `upgradeTower` command; the per-tick cap; and
  the compile check over every level (plus selectable targeting, if story 1 calls for it). Content
  stories bump `rulesetHash` only.
- **Parity comes first.** Before the bump re-pins anything, the M3 fixture re-expressed in format 4
  must reproduce sv18 on the M3 golden replay tick for tick in every state column — world-hash,
  terminal outcome, score and final tick included. Because a level is read from spend and the
  tower row gains no column, this comparison covers all state, which is stronger than M3 story 3's
  (whose new creep column sat outside it).
- **Goldens:** `m2-golden` and `m3-golden` keep running against their frozen fixtures. A
  no-upgrade run hashes as it did at sv18, so neither should move at the bump. The M4 golden (A6)
  joins the determinism-version guard at story 6.
- **No new RNG consumer;** ADR 0010 is untouched (the stun roll's count and order don't depend on
  its chance).
- **Unchanged:** the replay envelope, which carries no version key of its own; the playtrace
  payload's shape (`PLAYTRACE_VERSION` 1), whose serializer is exhaustive, so `upgradeTower` is a
  compile error until it is handled; and ADR 0008, since there is no resume and key bindings don't
  persist.
- **Bounds discipline:** every story that invalidates a compile-time or resident-state bound
  re-derives it in the same story — the tower-command budget, the per-tick cap and the run-length
  check at story 2.

### Completeness boundary

(a) **Deliberately not pinned, by class:** the same-tick ordering of an upgrade among other
commands beyond "input order"; how a level is derived and stored in code, provided the parity above
holds; how a pending upgrade is projected and named in the client; the reference-build notation;
the level cue's art and the Panel's wording; and, as tuning, every ladder step cost and magnitude,
every `hpPercent` from wave 21 on, and the checker's thresholds (the 40% and 15% upgrade shares,
the ≈ 90% spent share, the headroom shape).

(b) **Who fixes the residual:** story 2 fixes sim semantics in code and records them in its PR;
story 3 fixes presentation; stories 4–6 fix the numbers with the checker. Whether selectable
targeting ships is not a story's call: story 1 reports the screening reading, and Rob decides.

(c) **What arbitrates:** the **M4 determinism golden** and its `simVersion` pin decide tick-level
behaviour; the **format-4 schema and capability profile** decide the data shape; the **format-4
parity test** over the M3 fixture decides that a log with no upgrade is unchanged; the **checker's
pinned reference-build outcomes** decide balance claims. A review finding below this line is
answered by citing it.

## What M4 deliberately does not do

- New towers, creeps or effect primitives. Branching levels, or a level that changes a tower's
  role or domain.
- Timed upgrades. A relocate or move action.
- Selectable targeting priority, unless story 1's probe calls for it.
- Speed, armor or bounty creep leveling.
- Any economy re-tune, Easy and Hard, interest on savings, or the efficiency score input (all M5).
- Persistence or mid-run resume (Phase 2). Any new board.
- A maze solver; 3× / 4× speed; audio; automatic playtrace upload.
- Meta-progression, ever (PRD 0001 §3).

## Open questions / first-pass flags

Edges for reviewers — deliberate judgment calls, all tunable:

- **An escort can still screen its boss** (open from M3). Story 1's probe measures it; M3's content
  fixes come first, and selectable targeting is the third resort.
- **Upgrades may move board-full.** If the measured board-full wave leaves 24 ± 2, or upgrades never
  get bought before wave 24, the step costs move first, never the base costs.
- **The late game may have one obvious buy.** If the strongest build maxes its whole kill zone by
  wave 27 and banks the rest, or one tower gets maxed in every run, more levels or a capstone are
  the levers. Both are Rob's call, and either adds to the track rather than replacing it.
- **Upgrades may prove too weak at the fold.** Range growth is the next lever: in a maze it is the
  strongest late stat, because a longer reach covers more of each fold. But it changes the range
  ring a player has learned, makes a level-3 tower at the fold dominant, needs a format change and
  re-opens the perf question.
- **The block-3 tail is a first guess.** If the serpentine shows no rise in pressure through waves
  24–30, or both 1★ winners reach 3★, the tail steepens, or rises more on the armored, air and boss
  waves (24, 26 and 28–30) than on 25 and 27.
- **Anti-air may still crowd the winners.** If every winner's anti-air share stays above about 30%
  with upgrades, the both-domain `basic` is the next lever, and Rob's call. Under sticky "first" a
  both-domain `basic` near an air lane would chase flyers, as `slow` does (m2.md), and it would
  re-tune every pinned build.
- **"Max level" on a brand-new mine may read as a missing feature** to a newcomer. Seven of nine
  towers level by ruling; Rob's runs and the outside players are where it would show.
- **A raised per-tick cap enlarges what a hostile log can carry:** up to 512 inputs on every tick.
  Tower commands stay capped across the match either way; story 2 records the server's size bound.
