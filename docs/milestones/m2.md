## What M2 is

M2 is the **breadth** milestone (roadmap; PRD 0001's activation row): the sim's combat vocabulary
completes and the catalogs open up — **more tower types, more creep types (including air +
anti-air), more waves** — with the **wave-clear and early-call bonuses active** and the
**early-call scoring input live**, all on **M1's single-path board**. It is a public milestone
build — the **`p1-m2` checkpoint**, not a release — under the full accessibility gate (ADR 0003)
and externalized strings (ADR 0004), like every milestone.

PRD 0001 fixed the shapes; this spec fills in M2's **numbers and remaining semantics** so there is
**nothing to guess during implementation**. Every quantity is a **first-pass value, chosen to be
tuned** — content changes bump `rulesetHash`, never code. Terminology is the glossary's
([CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md)); this milestone's grill added four canonical terms there (**Effect
primitive**, **Wave preview**, **Boss**, **Early call**) and clarified **Domain**.

**The M2/M3 boundary, stated plainly:** M2's ~10-wave arc is a **showcase sequence** — each new
threat introduced once, brief combined pressure, a boss finale — proving the breadth vocabulary.
M3 then delivers **the** full _tuned_ campaign on the **two-path board** (the roadmap's R1 target
geometry), superseding this sequence. M2 deliberately does not attempt M3's pacing, difficulty
curve, or board.

## Concrete spec

### Board

Unchanged from M1: the 28 × 24 single-path board (`m1.md` §Board), entrance col 0 row 11 → exit
col 27 row 11, centre-leak, same cell classes and maze invariant. Flying creeps traverse the
straight entrance→exit line above all of it (below).

### The towers

Nine catalog entries. **A tower is a data-defined bundle of effect primitives** (glossary: Effect
primitive); each **attacking** bundle carries a target-domain mask (`ground` / `air` / `both`);
a support-only bundle has **no target domain**. `basic` is M1's tower, unchanged.

| id             | Effects (authored order)                     | Domain | Cost | Damage | Range (tiles)  | Cadence (ticks) | travel_ticks | Notes                                      |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------ | ---- | ------ | -------------- | --------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| `basic`        | direct (single-target)                       | ground | 5    | 10     | 4              | 30              | 4            | M1 baseline, unchanged                     |
| `splash`       | direct (AoE, radius 1.5)                     | ground | 12   | 8      | 4              | 60              | 8            | long lob — dodgeable by design             |
| `slow`         | direct (single-target) + slow 50% / 40t      | both   | 8    | 2      | 4              | 30              | 2            | the both-domain tower; soft anti-air       |
| `stun`         | direct (single-target) + stun 25% / 20t      | ground | 10   | 4      | 3              | 40              | 2            | chance-based (sim RNG)                     |
| `venom`        | direct (single-target) + DoT 4 per 10t / 60t | ground | 9    | 2      | 4              | 30              | 2            | DoT bypasses armor                         |
| `beacon`       | support: adjacent damage ×1.5                | —      | 15   | —      | — (adjacency)  | —               | —            | no attack; no target domain                |
| `mine`         | burst (AoE, radius 2.5), one discharge       | ground | 6    | 45     | 2.25 (trigger) | — (single use)  | 1            | consumed at fire tick; lands next tick     |
| `antiair`      | direct (single-target)                       | air    | 7    | 8      | 5              | 20              | 2            | the dedicated air answer                   |
| `frost-splash` | direct (AoE, radius 1.5) + slow 30% / 30t    | ground | 16   | 6      | 4              | 60              | 8            | **the combo — pure data, zero sim change** |

Per-effect semantics (all first-pass; PRD 0001 §4 shapes):

- **AoE (direct damage's area form):** point-locked; for a **cadenced** AoE tower (`splash`,
  `frost-splash`), the impact point leads the target along its current route at its fire-time
  effective speed, clamped to the exit. **`mine` is the exception** — a burst tower's blast is
  never led; see the Burst entry below for where it detonates instead. Blast membership is the
  Euclidean point-distance test at the impact tick, radius in fixed-point tiles, **boundary
  inclusive** (`dist ≤ radius`, both in integer fixed-point — the pinned rounding).
- **Slow:** a fixed-point speed multiplier (`slow 50%` = ×128/256; `30%` = ×179/256), floor
  rounding, **floored at ⌈25% of the creep's base speed⌉**; duration refresh per the stacking rules
  (strongest magnitude wins; a weaker application neither extends nor overrides).
- **Stun:** halts movement for the duration; **overrides slow while active**; the 25% chance is one
  sim-RNG draw per application attempt (threshold 64/256), and an **immune target consumes no
  draw** (immunity filters before the roll).
- **DoT:** ticks every 10 ticks starting 10 ticks after application; **bypasses armor**; per-source
  independence (two `venom` towers = two DoTs); a re-application from the same source refreshes
  duration and adopts the new fire-time magnitude — **the tick cadence stays anchored to the
  first application and a refresh never resets or suppresses it**, so a DoT tick due on the
  refresh tick still applies (at the newly adopted magnitude, since impacts resolve before DoT
  ticks); **source identity = tower instance id** — a sold source's DoT keeps ticking but can no
  longer refresh. That means no _new_ shots: an impact already fired before the sale still
  applies and still refreshes when it lands, the same fire-time-snapshot authority that already
  governs damage and a blast's lead prediction.
- **Support:** recipients are **attacking** towers whose 2×2 footprint shares **≥ 1 full cell
  edge** with the beacon's (corner-only touch excluded; beacons do not buff beacons — no
  chaining). Buffed field: **damage amounts — direct (single-target and AoE) and DoT per-tick magnitude** — ×384/256 (+50%), **floor-rounded to an integer** (an odd-damage tower buffs down — the same rounding rule as slow). The buff enters via the fire-time snapshot, so a buffed `venom` hits harder directly **and its next application carries a stronger DoT** (4 → 6 per tick) — PRD §4's re-buffed-source rule, verbatim. Non-damage magnitudes and durations (slow %, stun chance/duration) are never buffed. **Overlapping auras: the strongest single aura
  wins — no stacking** (the same shape as the status stacking rule). Buffs enter a shot via the
  fire-time snapshot; adjacency recomputes deterministically on every build/sell.
- **Burst:** arms on placement; fires its one AoE discharge at the first tick a target is inside
  its **trigger range** (2.25 tiles — amended from this doc's first-authored 2 tiles; see
  "Edges for reviewers" below for why), and the tower is **consumed at that fire tick**. The
  blast is **centred on the mine's own footprint** — it detonates in place and never leads its
  target, unlike a cadenced AoE tower (see the AoE entry above) — and its radius (2.5 tiles)
  deliberately **exceeds** the trigger range, so the creep that trips it is always inside the
  kill zone. Routing is per-tick derived state: the fire tick's own movement step **precedes** the consumption (combat
  runs after movement) and correctly sees the mine still standing; **every movement step after
  the consumption — beginning with the very next movement phase — uses the post-consumption
  maze** (PRD §3's re-route with no stale step thereafter); the pinned tick-level contract is **one routing snapshot per
  tick**, derived before movement — movement, targeting, and AoE lead all read it, and a
  mid-combat-phase consumption never re-derives mid-phase (towers firing later that combat phase
  read pre-consumption distances). The discharge itself resolves from its fire-time snapshot. A
  pre-fire `mine` sells for the normal 75% refund; a fired one is gone (no refund).

### The creeps

Eight catalog entries. **Creeps vary along the five axes** (speed, durability = HP + armor,
domain, effect-immunities, role); every entry carries a bounded **`leakCost`** (how many lives a
leak costs). `normal` is M1's creep, unchanged.

| id              | HP  | Speed (fp/tick) | Armor | Domain | Immunities | Role | leakCost | Kill bounty | Answers / answered by                         |
| --------------- | --- | --------------- | ----- | ------ | ---------- | ---- | -------- | ----------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| `normal`        | 20  | 26              | 0     | ground | —          | —    | 1        | 1           | M1 baseline                                   |
| `swarm`         | 7   | 30              | 0     | ground | —          | —    | 1        | 1           | dies to one `splash` blast — AoE's showcase   |
| `fast`          | 16  | 44              | 0     | ground | —          | —    | 1        | 2           | outruns fixed fire — `slow`'s showcase        |
| `armored`       | 36  | 22              | 6     | ground | —          | —    | 1        | 3           | blanks small hits — `venom`'s showcase        |
| `flying`        | 18  | 30              | 0     | air    | —          | —    | 1        | 2           | ignores the maze — `antiair`'s showcase       |
| `resolute`      | 20  | 44              | 0     | ground | slow       | —    | 1        | 2           | punishes slow-reliance — immunity's showcase  |
| `armored-flyer` | 30  | 26              | 5     | air    | —          | —    | 1        | 3           | **the composed creep** — axes compose as data |
| `boss`          | 400 | 18              | 8     | ground | stun       | boss | **3**    | 25          | the finale; cannot be stun-locked             |

Axis semantics (first-pass; PRD 0001 §5 shapes):

- **Armor** is a flat per-hit reduction on **direct** hits only, floored at zero — armor ≥ damage
  fully negates the hit (`splash`'s 8 vs the boss's 8 → 0, deliberately: the boss demands DoT,
  support-buffed damage, or `mine`). DoT bypasses armor entirely.
- **Immunity flags** are boolean; an immune target neither receives the effect nor (for
  chance-based effects) consumes an RNG draw.
- **Flying** creeps travel the straight entrance→exit line, over towers, walls, and blocked cells;
  they are hit only by `air`/`both` towers, they **do not block placement** (the
  never-build-on-a-creep rule tests ground creeps only), and they never constrain the maze
  invariant. Their "route" for targeting distance and AoE lead is that straight line.
- **Boss** (glossary): role, not a kind of stat — high durability, wave centerpiece, and a leak
  costs `leakCost` lives (**3**), which may overshoot lives below zero (PRD §7).
- Every spawned creep carries its **wave identity** (which wave spawned it) — the per-wave clear
  bonus depends on it.

### Combat resolution order & status semantics (the pinned total order)

The tick's outer frame is the phase order as built: **input → wave → field-derivation → movement →
combat → resolution**. Within the combat phase, the cross-effect order is pinned (this is the
determinism-gated detail PRD §4 delegates to the combat build):

1. Impacts due this tick resolve in **queue insertion order** — itself deterministic because
   towers fire in tower-id order (the scheduling key).
2. Within one impact, the nesting is: **outer loop = affected creeps in creep-id order** (an AoE's
   radius set; a single target trivially); per creep, **direct-damage effects apply in the
   bundle's authored relative order → death check → surviving creeps receive status effects in the
   bundle's authored relative order**. A lethal hit applies no statuses. Loaders normalize bundles
   so this traversal is well-defined.
3. After all due impacts, **DoT ticks** resolve over creeps in creep-id order, per-source in
   application order.
4. One **dead-creep sweep** then removes every creep killed this phase and credits its kill
   bounty — impacts and DoTs mark deaths, a single deterministic compaction collects them.
5. **Target acquisition and tower firing** run over the post-sweep world in tower-id order; a
   tower whose held target died earlier this same phase may reacquire and fire this tick.
6. An **expiry sweep closes the combat phase**: every status record whose inclusive final tick
   is this tick is removed — movement, DoT ticks, and firing on that final tick all still
   observed the effect, and **expired records never survive a tick boundary** (end-of-tick
   serialized state carries no expired statuses).
7. **RNG draws occur only inside that traversal, in exactly its order** (creep-then-effect).
8. Status **durations end inclusively** (an effect applied at tick T for duration D is active
   through tick T + D, and its record is removed by that tick's expiry sweep — step 6). A target-locked impact on a dead/leaked creep is wasted; an AoE resolves
   against whoever is alive in radius at the impact tick.

### Waves & timing

**Multi-wave state model** (replaces M1's single `pre-wave | active`): the sim's **run phase**
(running / terminal) separates from a **per-wave lifecycle**. The **held state is out-of-sim**:
before Start the controller simply advances no ticks — the same mechanism as pause — so the sim
needs no held→running transition, the replay records no start command, and re-simulation
reproduces the first countdown from the sim's tick-0 initial state alone (PRD §2) — each wave tracks
`scheduled → counting-down → spawning → resolved`. Countdowns are **sequential** (PRD §2). The
**handoff lifecycle is one rule, pinned to the phase order**:

- When a wave launches (by expiry or early call) during tick T's wave phase, the next scheduled
  wave flips `scheduled → counting-down` **in that same wave phase**, with its countdown full.
- Its **first decrement happens on T+1's wave phase** — which is exactly PRD §2's "countdown
  begins on the tick after the previous wave launches."
  Consequences, each deterministic:

- **End-of-tick invariant, no exceptions:** at every tick boundary, exactly one wave is counting
  down while any scheduled wave remains, and none once the final wave has launched. (The
  zero-countdown moment exists only inside the wave phase, never at an observable boundary.)
- The new countdown is **callable from its very first input phase (T+1) at maximum early-call
  value** — matching the listed reward maxima.
- **An early call applies in the input phase and immediately marks the counting-down wave launch-pending
  (uncallable from that command on);** the launch itself — spawns, bonus payment, credit, the
  next wave's flip — executes in that tick's wave phase. A second same-tick call therefore finds
  nothing callable and no-ops (PRD §2's anti-chain rule), and the bonus can never pay twice.

Countdowns run
**while prior waves' creeps are still on the board** — overlapping waves are the norm. A wave is **resolved** when its spawn schedule is
exhausted and none of its creeps remain alive.

**The arc — 10 waves** (composition · spacing · countdown before launch · clear bonus):

Mixed waves spawn their entries as **concurrent streams**: by default every stream's first
spawn is on the launch tick (offset 0), each stream continues on its own spacing, and
simultaneous spawns order deterministically by table-row order (wave 10: the boss on the launch
tick, escorts every 20 ticks from it). Per-stream offsets are schedule data that may override
the default. When spawns from **different overlapping waves** fall due on the same tick, the
**earlier-launched wave spawns first**, then table-row order within each wave — pinned because
spawn order assigns creep ids, which drive every id-based tie-break downstream.

**S11's balance pass (Stories § item 11) moved three levers off this table's first-pass
values, and touched no composition cell.** Wave 10's `normal` escort stream carries a
per-stream `offsetTicks` override of **600** (was 0 — the escort no longer spawns on the
boss's own launch tick, which had been screening the boss from being targeted first); the
`antiair` tower's `cadenceTicks` moved 20 → 15 (catalog data, not this table, but it is what
makes wave 8 survivable); and `scoring.survivalMul` moved 35 → 50 (the win/loss margin
lever). Every composition cell, every other spacing/countdown/clear-bonus cell in the table
above is exactly as first authored — frozen per ruling 2.

**Wave preview** (glossary; PRD §2 as amended): the countdown UI shows the **next wave's
composition, derived from the ruleset wave schedule** (authoritative — never duplicated UI data):
aggregated counts per creep kind in schedule order, each entry exposing **name (i18n key), count,
domain, armor, and immunities** as accessible text (never color/icon-only — ADR 0003). One wave of
lookahead only. After the final wave launches, the preview shows an explicit last-wave state.
(Amended at S10, Rob, 2026-08-08: the entry gains a sixth always-present accessible
member, **leak cost**, mirroring `armor`'s own always-shown slot — Act 1 ruling 3. The full
list is now name (i18n key), count, domain, armor, leak cost, and immunities.)

**Starting ≠ claiming (semantic pin, not a UI topology):** beginning the run banks nothing — the
first countdown simply starts ticking (it is callable from tick 0, PRD §2). The **early-call
reward is claimed only by a deliberate player act**. The recommended affordance is **one primary
control that morphs** ("Start" while held → "Call wave" for the rest of the run); the exact
affordance is the story's design call under ADR 0003 and the two-layouts contract. Today's Start
control enqueues `callWaveEarly` directly — that coupling must be gone **before** any nonzero
bonus or credit activates.

### Economy

- **Income sources now all live** (PRD §6): per-kill bounty (per the creep table), the per-wave
  **clear bonus** (table above), and the **early-call bounty bonus**.
- **Per-wave clear bonus:** paid **once**, at the wave's resolution tick — and within the
  resolution phase, **per-wave settlement (including this payment) precedes terminal
  evaluation**: a wave completing on the same tick the run ends, by win or by loss, still pays
  before the terminal is marked (one uniform rule; no loss short-circuit) — **forfeited if any of
  that wave's creeps leaked** (per-wave leak flag over the creep's wave identity — one wave's leak
  never forfeits another's bonus).
- **Early-call bounty bonus:** `⌊ticksRemaining / 50⌋` bounty (≈ 1 per 2.5 s shaved; up to 10 on
  wave 1, 6 on 300-tick countdowns). **Auto-launch pays 0.**
- **Starting bounty 80**, sell refund `⌊75% × cumulative spend⌋` — both unchanged from M1.
  Selling a `beacon` recomputes auras that tick; a fired `mine` cannot be sold (it no longer
  exists).

### Scoring & star grade

- **Numeric score** (outcome-dependent — product ruling, Rob 2026-07-26): on a **win**,
  `Σ kill-bounties + Σ early-call score credits + (max(0, lives_remaining) × 35)`; on a **loss**,
  `Σ kill-bounties` only — **early-call credits are forfeited at a loss terminal** (the tempo
  bet didn't pay off) and the survival term keeps the authoritative M1 scorer's **zero floor**
  (a multi-life boss overshoot never subtracts score). The ×35 survival multiplier is the
  **M2 ruleset's first-pass value** — M1's shipped ruleset keeps its ×25, and this value lands
  with S2's scorer story like every first-pass number in this spec (no simulator change in this
  docs PR). **Win-over-loss ordering is a pinned
  ruleset balance invariant:** the best achievable losing score must stay below the worst
  achievable winning score, verified by S11's balance pass and re-verified whenever numbers
  are retuned. With first-pass numbers: best loss = 201 (kill everything, leak ten 1-bounty
  creeps); worst win = 203 (leak the boss plus six 3-bounty creeps, finish on 1 life:
  168 + 35). The margin is deliberately thin at first-pass — S11 owns widening it.
  The **early-call score credit** (glossary: Early call) comes online per PRD §7:
  **`⌊ticksRemaining / 50⌋` score credit per deliberate early call** (≤ 10 on wave 1, ≤ 6 on 300-tick
  countdowns, ≤ 8 on the finale; ≈ 66 across a maximally aggressive run — meaningful beside the
  211 total kill bounty of the arc, roughly ⅓, not dominant). **Auto-launch earns 0.** Credit accrues in a dedicated cumulative counter beside
  `cumulativeKillBounty`; all additions are **guarded (saturating) within the safe-integer
  domain** (operands bounded: ticksRemaining ≤ 500).
- **Live HUD score accrues earned-so-far inputs** — kill bounty **and** early-call credit, the
  moment each is earned (product ruling, Rob 2026-07-26). The survival term remains
  **terminal-only**, and on a **loss** the authoritative terminal score excludes the forfeited
  credits — it can land **below** the last live readout. Deliberate, and consistent with the
  #53 ruling: the live score is earned-so-far, and no monotonicity is promised.
- **Star grade unchanged from M1** (3★ ≥ 9 lives · 2★ ≥ 6 · 1★ ≥ 1; loss = none) — thresholds
  re-tuned only if playtests demand (Open questions).

## Stories (the build plan)

Twelve stories, each ≈ one PR (S4 splits into two) with its own done-criteria, each delivering playable value. **Every
content story wires its evolving bundle into the shipped default** (client and server load the
updated artifact through S1's registry) — that is what makes each slice playable. `simVersion`
bumps at S2–S10 (nine intra-M2 bumps: milestone-build replays are valid within a build, not
across).

1. **Ruleset as data (#22).** The M1 ruleset moves to **schema-validated JSON** served to **both

## Determinism & data

- **Layering unchanged:** render/input read sim state, never mutate; all gameplay stays a pure
  function of `(seed, ruleset, boardId, tickInputs)` **under the replay's stamped `simVersion`**
  — the version selects the implementation, and the validator accepts only its own (ADR 0006).
- **simVersion cadence:** nine bumps (S2–S10) — each sim-shape change is one story's bump;
  milestone-build replays are valid within a build, never across. The `placeTower` change is gated by
  `simVersion` (ADR 0006 defines no separate replay version and M2 introduces none).
- **Schema policy:** the complete v2 structure is fixed at S1 (`formatVersion` stable through M2);
  the per-`simVersion` **capability profile** is the only thing that grows per story. Content
  changes bump `rulesetHash` per story (expected). M3's **richer geometry vocabulary** (multi-entrance,
  authored cell classes) is an acknowledged later `formatVersion` + `simVersion` evolution of
  the v2 bundle's board section — deliberately **not** pre-built.
- **ADR 0006 §2's stale parenthetical** ("when the previous wave clears") is corrected by a dated
  note in this PR: wave timing chains off the previous **launch** (PRD §2); the ADR's decision —
  spawns from the ruleset schedule inside the deterministic sim — stands.
- **Determinism completeness boundary.** This spec pins the tick-level semantics that shape
  design and cross-story contracts. It does **not** attempt to enumerate every remaining
  two-implementations-could-diverge micro-decision (record serialization orders, sweep
  placements not named above, further RNG/rounding corners): per PRD 0001's own delegation,
  those are **fixed by the first implementing story**, recorded in that story's PR, and locked
  by the **M2 determinism golden — the golden replay, not this prose, is the final arbiter of
  tick-level behavior.** Review findings of that class against this document are resolved by
  this paragraph.
- **Bounds discipline:** every story that invalidates a compile-time bound re-derives it in the
  same story (multi-wave bounds in S2; AoE scan caps in S4); every story that invalidates a
  resident-state bound does too (DoT state caps in S5 — a compiler ratio gate plus a rail
  derived from it, with only the source COUNT left downstream; see the S5 clause above and
  issue #77 — and resident-impact
  budgets as cadence/travel profiles change). The ADR 0005 spike is measured at S4 and
  re-measured as behaviors land; S11 runs the catalog-scale gate.

## Open questions / first-pass flags

Edges for reviewers — deliberate judgment calls, all tunable:

- **Support and burst ship — a decision PRD 0001 left open, taken here.** The PRD calls them
  "the most optional of the set — droppable if a leaner roster identity is wanted," and defers
  the call to "decide as their content lands." This is where they land, and M2 ships both:
  `beacon` (S8) and `mine` (S9) — because breadth means the combat vocabulary **completes**, and
  dropping either would falsify the cut line's "all six effect primitives." Burst stays the
  cheapest reversal in the spec (one story, one tower, nothing else depends on it), but re-opening
  it is a **decision, not a default**.
- **The beacon's aura is drawn as a footprint SHELL, not the ring this doc first specified —
  amended 2026-08-06 (Rob).** Two independent reasons, either sufficient. First, the adjacency
  rule is a square **edge share**, not a radius: a tower diagonally OFFSET from the beacon (at
  `(c+2, r+1)`) still shares a full cell edge while sitting farther from the centre than an
  edge-fitted circle would reach, so a circle draws a different rule than the one the sim
  enforces. Second, a second concentric circle on a tower footprint was already rejected in
  S4a's ship review as ambiguous — "is the inner one the splash, a permanent aura, or the
  range?" — with "a permanent aura" named as the exact confusion, and re-introducing it here
  would have re-opened a closed ruling. What ships instead is a rounded-rect shell one cell out
  from the 2×2 (bounding the stamped cells — it encloses the four excluded diagonal corners
  too, a recorded over-approximation; the ✦ carries the authoritative per-tower answer) plus a
  small ✦ on each buffed recipient, so the
  relationship reads from BOTH ends rather than from the beacon alone.
- **`mine`'s trigger and radius were re-ruled off this doc's first-authored numbers, and its
  damage held at 45 rather than the spec's 40 — amended 2026-08-07 (Rob).**
  - **Trigger 2.25 tiles, not the spec's 2.0** — a creep rounding the mine's 2×2 corner never
    gets closer than **2.121 tiles** (the diagonal elbow), so the authored 2.0 left a **silent
    dead zone**: a mine the maze routes diagonally past would sit inert forever, failing quietly
    rather than loudly.
  - **Radius 2.5 tiles, detonating in place, not the spec's 1.5 lead-the-target** — the mine is a
    **wall creeps route around**, so its footprint centre (on a cell corner in both axes) is
    never nearer than **1.5 tiles** to any creep; the authored 1.5 caught orthogonal huggers only
    on the inclusive boundary and missed diagonal ones entirely.
  - **Damage 45, not 40** — at 40 the mine deals 34 through `armored`'s armor 6 and leaves it
    alive **on 2 creep HP**, a coincidence on the game's largest single hit that reads as a
    rounding bug. 45 deals 39 and kills with 3 to spare.
  - **A selected tower draws its blast spokes, on the same condition the ghost preview
    already uses** (`blastRadiusFp !== null`). The mine forced the question — it is the only
    tower whose blast (2.5 tiles) reaches past its own ring (2.25), so drawing the ring alone
    would understate it. This story first shipped a narrower gate (`blastRadiusFp > rangeFp`,
    satisfied only by the mine) chosen so that `splash` would be left untouched; the
    consequence was that arming a `splash` previewed spokes and selecting that same `splash`
    showed none — same tower, two pictures. **Re-ruled for consistency: `splash` and
    `frost-splash` DO gain a selection cue they did not draw before**, a deliberate change
    rather than a side effect.
  - All ruled by Rob, 2026-08-07.
- **The credit weights** (`/50` for both bounty and score, coinciding at first-pass) set
  maximally aggressive early-calling at ≈ ⅓ of the arc's
  kill score when played maximally aggressively. If playtests show call-spam dominating score,
  tune the divisor before touching the mechanic.
- **One `splash` does not clear the swarm wave — measured, and accepted as-is (Rob, 2026-07-30).**
  S4's done criterion reads "swarm wave clears with one well-placed `splash`". Measured against the
  shipped bundle, the best single `splash` leaks **5 of 16** `swarm`, halving lives; **two** towers
  clear it outright. The ruling was to change **no** first-pass numbers and to pin the measured
  outcome in the S4 story test, so the criterion stands as written but is **known not to hold at
  these numbers**. (The exhaustive placement sweep behind this sits in S4a's PR and story test,
  not here.) **Re-ruled at S11 (Rob, 2026-08-08), not retuned:** cranking `splash` cadence to
  satisfy the one-tower criterion would perturb waves 2, 5, and 9 alike, and the criterion itself
  was wrong — "one `splash` clears the wave" makes the wave free, contradicting what it teaches.
  Replaced with **"the swarm wave is clearable with AoE, and not clearable without it,"** measured
  in both directions in `story-swarm.test.ts`: two `splash` towers clear it outright (a single
  blast is the clearing mechanism), and an exhaustive sweep of every non-AoE attacking tower id at
  the same two-tower budget clears it with zero builds.
- **The ADR 0005 spike came back over budget on frame time — ruled 2026-07-31: real-device pass at S11, with the diagnosis to happen before it.**
  Measured at the ADR's own worst case (peak 304 concurrent creeps, median 224, 150 towers, blasts
  and slows live), as **95th-percentile frame time**, the statistic the ADR names: **66.8 ms** on
  the low-end profile against a 33.3 ms budget (the 30 fps floor), and **25.6 ms** on mid-range
  against 16.7 ms (60 fps). Both trip the ADR's escalation trigger. Mid-range's **median** frame time is
  16.8 ms — on target — so that breach is a p95 tail at 39 fps, not a uniformly slow profile. `step()` costs 0.2–0.32 ms per
  **50 ms tick**, and the sim held its 20 Hz cadence throughout, so **the deterministic core is not the
  bottleneck** — the cost is in presentation. The emulation flatters rather than penalizes (a 6×
  CPU throttle on an M4 Pro with a Metal GPU is not a low-end webview), and the specced arc never
  reaches 300 concurrent creeps, so this bounds the ceiling
  scene, not real M2 play. ADR 0005 calls this the signal to revisit the Phaser bet; S4b measured
  it and deliberately did not diagnose it. See ADR 0005's 2026-07-30 amendment and

- **The `step()` ratio gate is noisier than its own ceiling — ruled 2026-07-31: accept the flake, with the rate on record.**
  `R = p99(stress due-blast) / p50(control)` — the numerator as it stood at the time; it moved to
  p95 in the 2026-08-03 amendment below, and to p50 at M2-S6 — was designed so a ratio would cancel runner speed and
  transfer anywhere. It does
  not. `R0` had to be re-recorded on the runner (1.69 → 2.49) after the local baseline failed CI,
  and the branch then measured **eight** CI samples spanning **2.3585–3.2478 (37.7%)** — five of
  them on _byte-identical code_. One sits **above** the 3.1125 ceiling the committed `R0` creates
  and another ~2.5% under it. Stated precisely: the shipped ceiling has been **evaluated three
  times and passed all three**, but **one of the eight samples would have failed it** — "1-in-8"
  is that counterfactual, not an observed failure rate. `R0` was **not** moved again at the time (the
  median of all eight, 2.55, changed no sample's verdict), and `TOLERANCE` was not widened:
  the worst sample sat 30.4% above `R0`, so absorbing it needs `TOLERANCE ≥ 1.31`, and a gate
  that permits a 31% regression before complaining is not worth the CI minutes. `perf` is **not** a
  required check, so a flake is noise rather than something that stops a merge — but noise is what
  teaches people to ignore a gate, which is the real risk here. The owner's options were: accept
  the flake rate — which is the ruling. A dedicated runner costs infrastructure for a job that is
  not required. Revisit if it flakes in practice; expect roughly 1 run in 8. Full record in `packages/perf/src/gate.ts`'s `R0` doc and
  `docs/design-notes/performance-spike.md`.

  **AMENDED 2026-08-03 (M2-S5b P11).** `R` moved from p99 to p95 on the numerator, and `R0` was
  re-recorded to **1.42** (median of five CI samples on the post-P9 workload, run 30851346335
  attempts 1–5, `ubuntu-24.04`; ceiling 1.7750). This is **not** the "R0 is not being moved again"
  sentence above being reversed under pressure: that sentence was a same-workload, same-statistic

  them. The declined **p99/p99 alternative** above is superseded, not revived: what was actually
  adopted is p95 on the numerator, on the strength of a pinned injected-regression fixture
  (`packages/perf/src/gate-fixture.test.ts`, catching a broad blast-cost regression at `k = 0.020`
  at every legal subset size where p99 caught none) — not the three-local-runs reasoning this
  finding rightly rejected. A finding from the same five-sample re-record belongs on record here:
  p95's spread across the five R values is **20.2%** (`(max-min)/min`), nearly double the **11.1%**
  spread of p99 over the exact same five runs — the opposite of what this file's own
  tail-noise diagnosis predicts. The switch does not rest on that prediction; it rests on the fired
  fixture. See `gate.ts`'s `R0` doc and ADR 0005's amended Finding 3 for the full record.

  **AMENDED 2026-08-05 (M2-S6 QC) — this amendment SUPERSEDES the 2026-08-03 p95 state
  above.** The numerator is now
  p50 over the same due-blast subset, `TOLERANCE` is 1.10, and `R0` is 1.00 (ceiling 1.1000,
