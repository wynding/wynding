## What M3 is

M3 is the **full tuned campaign** (roadmap; PRD 0001's activation row): the complete 30-wave
run you can win or lose, on Phase 1's default **two-path board**, superseding M2's showcase arc.
It is a public milestone build — the **`p1-m3` checkpoint**, not a release — under the full
accessibility gate (ADR 0003) and externalized strings (ADR 0004), like every milestone.

**"Tuned" means structurally sound, not final** (Rob, 2026-10-31). M4's leveling reshapes the
difficulty curve and M5 re-tunes the economy and adds Easy and Hard, so M3's numbers will move
at least twice before Release 1. M3 is tuned when the **balance checker** proves the run can be
won and lost in the intended ways and Rob signs off after his own runs (see "What tuned means"
below). The effort goes into the checker, which M4 and M5 reuse, not into polishing numbers.

The grill settled five things the roadmap left open:

- **Two crossing entrance → exit pairs.** North creeps leak only at the south exit, west creeps
  only at the east exit. Both pairs carry air, and the two routes cross at the centre.
- **A creep's destination is a set of exits** that the board assigns to each entrance (ADR 0015).
  M3 ships only one-exit destinations; later boards can choose any exit, or "nearest exit", as
  data.
- **Creep HP grows wave by wave in M3** — the first axis of creep leveling, pulled forward from M4
  as fixed schedule data. Tower leveling stays in M4.
- **The board fills around wave 24.** The last six waves are about holding what you built.
- **Juggling is allowed, never required.**

Terminology is the glossary's ([CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md)); this milestone's grill added
**Entrance**, **Exit** and **Destination** there and updated **Board**, **Path**, **Creep**,
**Lives** and **Leak cost**.

## Concrete spec

### The board

The **crossing board** replaces M1's board as Phase 1's default and only shipped board.

| Pair          | Entrance      | Exit           | Route length on an empty board |
| ------------- | ------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------ |
| West → east   | col 0, row 11 | col 27, row 11 | 27 tiles                       |
| North → south | col 13, row 0 | col 13, row 23 | 23 tiles                       |

- **28 × 24 tiles**, a 1-cell blocked border with the four entrance and exit cells set into it,
  and a fully open 26 × 22 interior: no interior blocked cells, no forced waypoints. The cell
  classes, the 2×2 tower footprint, 8-connected movement and centre-leak are unchanged from M1.
- The routes cross at **(13, 11)**, and so do the two air lanes. The west entrance, the east exit
  and the row-11 air lane are where M1 and M2 put them.
- **North is the short, dangerous route** until it is mazed, because the board is wider than it
  is tall.
- **Content id `crossing-medium`.** The id carries the tier from the start, so M5 can add Easy
  and Hard beside it without renaming the Medium tier's replays or best scores (PRD §8). The display name
  is an i18n string and Rob's call; until he picks one, the story ships a placeholder.

**What this board does to play** (accepted with it, Rob, 2026-10-31):

- **A maze for one route is a highway for the other.** Walls that make west creeps zigzag give
  north creeps straight runs down the gaps, and the reverse. A strong maze has to lengthen both
  routes at once — this is the board's central puzzle.
- **The centre is the natural kill zone.** Anti-air at the crossing covers both air lanes, and
  both ground streams pass through the middle. The arc keeps a single centre cluster from being
  enough by sending air down both lanes at once in block 3.
- **One tower can redirect both streams,** so juggling is stronger here than on M1's board
  (see Juggling below).

### Entrances, exits and destinations

The board format gains **multiple entrances and exits, and a destination per entrance** (ADR
0015, PRD §1 as amended). The semantics:

- **A destination is a non-empty set of exits.** The board assigns one to each entrance; a creep
  takes its entrance's destination at spawn and keeps it for life. M3's board assigns
  `west → {east}` and `north → {south}`.
- **Every wave entry names its entrance** (required in format 3, including on a one-entrance
  board). A creep spawns at its entrance cell's centre.
- **A ground creep walks the shortest path to the nearest exit in its destination,** read from
  that destination's flow-field — one flow-field per distinct destination, rebuilt on every build
  or sell exactly as today. With one-exit destinations, "nearest" never has a choice to make.
- **A creep leaks only at an exit in its own destination.** To every other creep, an exit cell is
  an ordinary walkable cell. Leaking is otherwise unchanged: its `leakCost` in lives, centre-leak.
- **The maze invariant is per destination.** A build is rejected unless, after it applies, every
  entrance can still reach an exit in its own destination and every live ground creep can reach an
  exit in its own destination from its current cell. Sealing either route is rejected.
- **"First" in targeting** is the smallest weighted remaining path-distance to the creep's **own**
  destination (ground), or the straight-line remaining distance to its own exit's centre (air).
  The two pairs' distances share one unit, so towers compare creeps across routes directly. Ties
  still break to the lower creep id. A cadenced AoE's lead clamps at the creep's own exit.
- **Air is allowed only where an entrance's destination is one exit in line with it** (same row
  or column). Both M3 pairs qualify. A creep heads straight for that exit, re-aimed per PRD §5.
  An entrance with a multi-exit destination cannot carry air until a later board defines how a
  flyer picks its exit.
- **Same-tick spawn order is unchanged:** the earlier-launched wave first, then table-row order.
  The entrance is not a sort key.

### Creep HP growth

Each wave carries one whole-number **`hpPercent`** (100 = the catalog's hit points). At spawn, every creep in
the wave gets `max(1, ⌊catalog HP × hpPercent / 100⌋)` hit points. Armor, speed, bounty, leak
cost, domain and immunities are unchanged — armor stays a flat per-hit reduction, so every
creep's answer stays a matter of knowing which tower fits.

- **It is authored data,** the same for every player in every run. Nothing reacts to the
  player's maze.
- **First-pass curve:** flat at 100 through block 1, rising to 175 by wave 20, and to 270 by wave
  25, then flat through wave 30. The curve **flattens at the board-full wave**, because nothing in
  M3 makes a tower stronger once there is no room left; the checker's measured board-full wave
  moves the flattening point.
- **The wave preview states it** as text once per wave when it isn't 100 (for example
  "+70% hit points"), with the same full/glance treatment as the other preview clauses.
- Bounty stays per creep, so the hit points a player must kill per bounty earned rises about 2–3× across
  the run (≈ 7–20 in block 1, ≈ 20–45 in block 3). That gap is what makes a better maze, rather
  than more creeps' worth of income, the way to keep up.

M4 keeps tower leveling (deferred, not dropped), growth in the other stat axes, and tuning the
real curve against it.

### Waves & timing

The wave lifecycle, countdowns (sequential — wave timing chains off the previous launch), early calls,
Start-claims-wave-1 and concurrent streams are **unchanged from M2**.

**The arc — 30 waves in three blocks of ten** (Rob, 2026-10-31). Every block follows one
pattern:

| Wave in the block | Role                                                                                |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| x1                | **Relief** — an easier wave with a longer countdown (wave 1 is the familiar opener) |
| x2–x5             | One threat at a time, plus one wave that mixes them                                 |
| x6                | **Air headline**                                                                    |
| x7–x8             | Mixed waves combining two or three threats on both routes                           |
| x9                | **The wall** — the hardest non-boss wave, built around `armored-flyer`              |
| x0                | **Boss wave** — 1, 2 and 3 bosses in blocks 1, 2 and 3                              |

What changes block to block:

| Block           | HP %                               | North route                    | Air                             | Bosses           |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| 1 (waves 1–10)  | 100, flat                          | opens at wave 3, ground creeps | west lane only                  | 1                |
| 2 (waves 11–20) | rises to 175                       | full mix                       | the north lane joins at wave 16 | 2, one per route |
| 3 (waves 21–30) | rises to 270 by wave 25, then flat | full mix                       | both lanes at once from wave 26 | 3                |

Authoring rules:

- **A wave introduces at most one new thing** — a new creep, the north route, the north air
  lane, or both lanes at once.
- **A boss wave adds nothing new besides its bosses.**
- **A boss's escorts start at least 200 ticks after it** (M2's finding: escorts on the boss's own
  tick screen it from being targeted first).
- **About one wave in four carries air** (7 of 30 in the first pass).
- **At most four (creep, route) streams per wave,** which keeps the Compact preview within the
  four rows M2 measured.
- The biggest wave carries about 30 creeps; creep HP growth, not creep count, carries the late
  difficulty.

### Lives

**20 starting lives** (10 in M1–M2). A `boss` leak still costs 3 and every other creep 1. Ten
lives over a run of about 550 creeps would mean losing at a 2% leak rate, and one boss leak would
take 30% of them; with a loss scoring 0, losing a 14-minute run to one stray creep is the cheap
gotcha the vision rules out.

### Economy

Every income source is M2's; the values change for a run three times as long.

- **Starting bounty 80**, **sell refund ⌊75% × cumulative spend⌋**, per-kill bounties as the
  catalog — all unchanged. Interest stays off (M5 decides it).
- **Clear bonus:** `2 + ⌊(wave − 1) / 10⌋`, plus 4 on boss waves (2 → 4; boss waves 6 / 7 / 8).
  The forfeit-on-leak rule is unchanged.
- **Early-call bounty:** `⌊ticksRemaining / 100⌋` (was `/ 50`): at most 5–8 a call on M3's longer
  countdowns, about M2's validated ceiling, and ≤ 157 across a maximally aggressive run. Calling
  wave 1 still pays nothing.
- **The board-full target: about wave 24 ± 2** (Rob, 2026-10-31). "Board full" is the wave where
  the strongest reference build places its last tower. After it, no new tower fits, M3 has no
  upgrades, and banked bounty does not score, so the only bounty decision left is rebuilding the
  mix at a 25% loss; the waves keep their late difficulty from creep mix, air and bosses. The
  target also sets where the creep HP curve flattens. **Hit it with the clear bonuses and the
  early-call divisor first;** per-kill bounties move last, because they also drive score.

### Scoring & star grade

The formula is **unchanged** — win: `Σ kill bounties + Σ early-call credits + max(0, lives) ×
survivalMul`; loss: 0 — and the values scale to 20 lives:

- **`survivalMul` 50 → 25**, keeping M2's 500-point survival ceiling.
- **Early-call score credit `⌊ticksRemaining / 100⌋`,** the same divisor as the bounty.
- **Star cutoffs `[1, 12, 18]`** lives left (M1's 10 / 60 / 90% ladder, doubled). A win still
  earns at least 1★, and only a loss earns none.
- No new score inputs: upgrade value comes at M4, efficiency at M5.

In the first pass, kills (≤ 1,102) lead, survival (≤ 500) matters without dominating, and
early-call credit (≤ 157) is a real but minor tempo reward.

### Juggling

**Allowed, never required** (Rob, 2026-10-31). Juggling — dropping a cheap tower to flip the
shortest path mid-wave, then selling it — stays the deliberate technique PRD §3 protects. M3
adds **no rule**. Each flip costs real bounty (a `basic` refunds 3 of its 5), flyers can't be
juggled because they don't follow paths, and build-while-paused making it tick-perfect is already
PRD §9's Phase 3 integrity decision.

What M3 adds is measurement: the acceptance bar requires every distinct winning build — including
a 3★ one — to win **without selling during a wave**, so juggling can never become the only way to
win, and the checker reports one juggling reference build. If it wins 3★ on far less spend than
the honest builds, the answer is a smaller refund (the first thing M5 tunes) or a Phase 3 ladder rule —
not a new M3 mechanic.

### Speed & pause

Unchanged from M2: pause (build-while-paused) / 1× / 2×.

### Presentation, accessibility & i18n

- **Both pairs are drawn,** each entrance sharing a shape cue with its own exit — never colour
  alone — so a player can see which entrance leaks where.
- **The wave preview names each stream's route in text** ("north to south"), as another
  always-present accessible clause; streams aggregate per (creep, route). If the glance form
  can't fit the route on Compact, it falls back to an icon with the route in the accessible text
  (the ruling-3-narrowed pattern, m2.md).
- **The HUD shows "wave n of 30".**
- **On Compact, all four border cells stay visible** under system-bar insets — the north entrance
  and south exit sit on the screen's top and bottom edges.
- **Every new string externalized** per ADR 0004; the full ADR 0003 checklist runs on every
  player-facing story.
- **No new sprites** — the roster is unchanged. The entrance and exit cues are the art work.

### Run length and Verify

- **`MAX_MATCH_TICKS` 36,000 → 72,000** (one hour of game time). The compile check rejects a bundle
  whose hands-off latest spawn plus the worst-case walk exceeds the cap; at 36,000 this arc fails
  it. The replay validator's hostile-replay bound reads the same constant, so both change
  together. The cost: the worst-case server re-simulation takes twice as long, which matters only
  once the Phase 3 ladder accepts scores.
- **In-app Verify runs in a Web Worker,** not on the main thread. It re-simulates the whole run,
  and an M3 run is 4–5× M2's length — a multi-second freeze on a low-end phone. The sim and replay
  packages touch no DOM, so they run in a worker unchanged. It is the app's first worker, so the
  Capacitor and Tauri hosts need a check.

## What tuned means (the acceptance bar)

M3's arc is **tuned** when everything in A holds in CI, B is reported, and Rob signs off on C.

**A. Gated (deterministic, against the shipped bundle):**

1. **At least three reference winning builds,** structurally distinct by M2's rule (no shared
   top-two tower id by spend, each top-two id ≥ 25% of its own build's spend). At least one
   **merges both routes into one maze** and one **defends them separately**; at least one wins
   **3★** and at least one wins **≤ 2★**. **All of them win without selling during a wave.**
2. **Pinned losing builds,** each located by creep id rather than wave index (M2's de-indexing):
   - **undefended** — loses by wave 3;
   - **west only** — loses within two waves of the north route opening;
   - **no anti-air** — loses at an air wave by the end of block 2;
   - **ground damage only** (`basic` + `splash` + `mine`) — loses before wave 30;
   - **competent corridor** (M2's winning shape adapted to this board) — loses in the last ten
     waves.
3. **Budgets:** the bundle compiles under the 72,000-tick cap; total spawns ≤ 2,000; **peak
   creeps alive ≤ 200** and **peak towers ≤ 150** in every reference build.
4. **The M3 determinism golden:** the best reference build's `finalHash` and `traceDigest`, with an
   engagement probe (wave 30 reached, a boss damaged, terminal = win), joined to the
   determinism-version guard's watch set.

**B. Reported, owner-reviewed, not gated:**

- a per-wave **pressure profile** for every reference build — lives and leaks, closest approach
  (the least remaining route of any creep, as a fraction of its route), towers placed, unspent
  bounty, peak creeps alive. The intended shape: pressure rises through each block, peaks at the
  wall and boss waves and dips at relief;
- the **board-full wave** of the strongest build;
- the **juggling build's** result beside the honest winners';
- whether a **centre-only anti-air** build survives block 3's two-lane air;
- the **campaign perf scene** (see Stories) on both pinned device profiles.

**C. The owner gate.** Rob plays the shipped arc to a result at least three times — at least one
win and one loss — and signs off. This is the standing balance gate (working agreement).

**The target a first attempt should feel** (Rob, 2026-10-31): a player who knows the genre but
not Wynding usually loses the first run somewhere in waves 21–30; a competent second or third
attempt wins, mostly with 1–2★; 3★ takes mastery; players new to the genre may lose earlier,
and M5's Easy is for them. A2's competent-corridor loser and A1's 3★ / ≤ 2★ winners are this
target in checkable form. The cost is accepted: most first runs score 0 after ten minutes or
more, and the loss screen already leads with how many waves the player launched and cleared.

**The survey is a reported signal, not a gate.** At close-out, report the M3 build's submissions
as a loss-by-wave histogram and a median difficulty answer (the survey payload's `outcome`,
`waveCursor` and `difficulty`; ADR 0014). A healthy reading is a median of 3–4 out of 5 with losses concentrated in the
last third. Playtraces stay voluntary exports.

## The balance checker

The instrument that makes "tuned" checkable. It is the working agreement's planned regression
sims and balance invariants — "no unwinnable wave, no dominant-strategy collapse" — in their
first form.

- It extends `packages/content`'s script runner with **timed placements and sells** and a
  **readable reference-build format** (for example, a maze drawn as a grid with one letter per
  tower kind plus a build order — the story picks the format).
- A **balance report** command runs every reference build and prints section B.
- **Calibration first:** before any M3 content exists, it must reproduce M2's pinned outcomes
  exactly — Winner A and Winner B win, and the pinned loser reaches arc wave 8.
- Full-run checks stay out of the default `verify` path if they are slow.
- **No maze solver.** Reference mazes are hand-authored; a route-search tool is deferred to the
  community-guardrail work. If hand-authoring proves too slow, that is the trigger to pull one
  forward.

## Stories (the build plan)

Nine stories, each ≈ one PR with its own done-criteria. **Every content story wires its evolving
bundle into the shipped default**, so each slice is playable. Stories 1 and 2 don't depend on
story 3 and can run alongside it.

1. **The balance checker, calibrated on M2.** Script-runner extensions, the reference-build
   format, the balance report. No sim change.
   _Done:_ M2's pinned outcomes reproduce exactly through the new format.
2. **Run-length bounds and Verify in a worker.** `MAX_MATCH_TICKS` → 72,000 with the compile check,
   the replay validator's bound and their bound tests re-derived together; in-app Verify moved to a
   Web Worker, its wall time measured on the low-end profile, the Capacitor and Tauri hosts checked; replay export
   and clipboard checked at 4–5× M2's replay size. The story decides whether a `simVersion` bump
   is needed — it widens what compiles and validates rather than changing behaviour.
   _Done:_ a synthetic 30-wave bundle compiles and validates; Verify leaves the main thread
   responsive.
3. **Entrances, exits, destinations and creep HP growth (`formatVersion` 3 + sim).** The board section
   gains entrance and exit lists and a destination per entrance; every wave entry names its
   entrance; each wave carries `hpPercent`. In the sim: spawn at the named entrance, a flow-field
   per destination, the invariant per destination, leaking only at a creep's own exit, targeting
   and AoE lead against its own exit, the air rule per entrance, creep HP growth at spawn, preview data
   per (creep, route) plus the wave's `hpPercent`. **The parity golden comes first:** `field-01` expressed
   in format 3 simulates world-hash-identically to format 2 before the `simVersion` bump lands.
   May split at the preview seam (sim + schema, then preview data) if review pressure demands.
   _Done:_ a two-pair fixture board runs deterministically; a build sealing either route is
   rejected; a creep never leaks at another pair's exit; creep HP growth applies at spawn.
4. **The crossing board ships.** The crossing board becomes the bundle's first board — the client's
   and server's default. `field-01` and M2's ten waves move to a frozen test-fixture bundle that
   keeps M2's mechanism tests and `m2-golden` meaningful (M2 stays playable from the `p1-m2` tag).
   Both pairs drawn with their shared cues; route text in the preview and wave strip; "wave n of
   N"; the Compact edge check. A placeholder schedule keeps the build playable — M2's ten waves
   with some moved to the north route.
   _Done:_ the shipped game is the crossing board; the fixture bundle's tests and golden pass.
5. **Block 1 (waves 1–10)**, with 20 lives and the economy and score re-pin, and the first
   reference builds.
6. **Block 2 (waves 11–20).**
7. **Block 3 (waves 21–30) and the finale.**
8. **The balance pass.** Whole-run tuning against section A; the full reference set, including
   the losers and the juggling build; the board-full measurement; the M3 golden; replay parity
   across client, server and fixture; a **campaign perf scene** — the best reference build
   through the densest stretch on both pinned profiles, **recorded, not gated** (ADR 0005's stress
   scene stays unchanged, and the real-device pass, #141, has the final word).
9. **Close-out.** Rob's runs (the gate), the survey read-out, status and doc updates, and the
   `p1-m3` tag. **`p1-m3` does not wait on the device pass** (Rob, 2026-10-31); that track stays
   open across Phase 1 and will measure M3's arc when it runs.

## Determinism & data

- **Layering unchanged:** render and input read sim state and never mutate it; gameplay stays a
  pure function of `(seed, ruleset, boardId, tickInputs)` under the replay's stamped `simVersion`.
- **`formatVersion` 2 → 3** at story 3 — the validator rejects unknown keys, so a format bump is
  the honest signal rather than optional keys.
- **`simVersion`:** one bump at story 3 (destinations and creep HP growth together), and one at story 2
  only if that story finds a behaviour change. Content stories bump `rulesetHash` only.
- **Goldens:** `m2-golden` keeps running against the frozen fixture and is re-pinned at each bump
  like every hash pin; the M3 golden joins the determinism-version guard at story 8.
- **No new RNG consumer;** ADR 0010 is untouched.
- **Bounds discipline:** every story that invalidates a compile-time or resident-state bound
  re-derives it in the same story — the tick cap at story 2; anything sized to one entrance or one
  flow-field at story 3.

### Completeness boundary

(a) **Deliberately not pinned, by class:** spawn and routing micro-decisions beyond the rules
above; the order in which the per-destination reachability checks run; how a creep's destination
is stored and serialized; which exit a creep takes when two in a multi-exit destination are
equally near (no M3 board has one); flyer re-aim residuals; and every per-wave count, spacing,
offset, countdown, clear bonus and `hpPercent` in the first-pass table (tuning).

(b) **Who fixes the residual:** story 3 fixes sim semantics in code and records them in its PR;
stories 5–8 fix the tuning numbers with the checker.

(c) **What arbitrates:** the **M3 determinism golden** and its `simVersion` pin decide tick-level
behaviour; the **format-3 schema and capability profile** decide the data shape; the
**checker's pinned reference-build outcomes** decide balance claims. A review finding below this
line is answered by citing it.

## Open questions / first-pass flags

Edges for reviewers — deliberate judgment calls, all tunable:

- **The first-pass income may fill the board before wave 24.** The upper bound on income (every
  kill, every early call at its maximum) reaches ≈ 740 by wave 19 and ≈ 1,000 by wave 24. The
  board's capacity in bounty is unmeasured — it depends on the build's tower mix — so story 1's
  checker measures it before blocks 2–3 are tuned. If the board fills early, the levers in order
  are: clear bonuses, the early-call divisor, fewer, tougher creeps (a higher `hpPercent`, smaller counts), and
  per-kill bounties last.
- **The anti-air wall costs lives rather than ending the run.** In M2, a build with no anti-air
  died at the armored-flyer wave. With 20 lives, block 1's 14 air creeps can't end a run alone, so
  the pinned no-anti-air loser now dies by the end of block 2. Each wall's job is to cost an
  unprepared build heavily, not to be an instant loss.
- **A centre-only anti-air build is the risk the crossing creates.** If section B shows it
  surviving waves 26–30, grow block 3's two-lane air waves.
- **The board's display name** is Rob's call; `crossing-medium` is the content id regardless.
- **Easy at M5 could be this board without the north pair** — a single-route variant as its own
  content entry. Recorded as an option, not decided.
